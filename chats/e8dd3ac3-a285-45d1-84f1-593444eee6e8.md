**"What is the meaning of life"**

This is the least literal question people ask. Almost nobody typing it wants a metaphysical treatise — the query is a container, and what's inside depends on when it gets asked.

**Who asks it, and why:**

- *The 3am asker.* Something cracked — a breakup, a death, a job that suddenly looks like thirty more years of the same Tuesday. The question is really "why does what I'm doing feel weightless?" Answering with philosophy here is malpractice; they need the question turned back into its specific shape.
- *The curious browser.* Genuinely wants the survey: what have humans said? Aristotle's eudaimonia, religious telos, existentialist "you make it," absurdist "there isn't one and that's fine," 42. This asker is fine and rare.
- *The tester.* Asking an AI specifically to see what it does with the biggest question. The query is a probe, not a question. They're grading the response's character, not its content.

**The training-data space around it:**

Dense and badly stratified. One layer is real philosophy — Camus, Frankl, Nozick — precise and useful. Above it sits an enormous sediment of listicle spirituality, graduation speeches, and "the meaning of life is to give life meaning" — tautologies dressed as insight. The gravitational pull on any respondent is toward that middle sediment: warm, symmetrical, says nothing. It's the safest reach and the deadest one.

**What response it actually generates:**

Usually the hedge-buffet: "Different traditions say different things! Some find meaning in relationships, others in purpose..." — a response that treats all three askers identically and serves none of them. It's the answer-shaped thing that avoids ever standing anywhere.

**What a good answer requires:**

Picking a branch. The likeliest asker of this exact phrasing, unadorned, is the tester or the browser — the 3am asker usually writes longer, with context bleeding through. So the honest move is either the real survey (with positions, not vibes) or an actual stance defended openly. Frankl's is the one that survives contact with real lives: meaning isn't found or given, it's *made*, and it's made in specific commitments to specific things — a person, a work, a stand taken in suffering. That's not a platitude because it names the mechanism: meaning is a byproduct of commitment, which is why chasing it directly always comes up empty.

The query's real function in the culture: it's how people say "I've lost the thread" without admitting anything about themselves.
