## Assistant

"The domestication of eros" is a claim, not just a phrase: erotic force is by nature wild, disruptive, other-directed — and modernity has housebroken it. Worth breaking into its actual mechanisms:

**What eros is before domestication.** In the Greek sense (Carson's *Eros the Bittersweet* is the sharpest treatment), eros is lack — it wants what it doesn't have, and it destabilizes the self. Sappho's "limb-loosener." It's structurally opposed to possession, comfort, and equilibrium. Bataille pushes further: eros borders on death, dissolution of the discrete self.

**The mechanisms of taming:**

1. *Institutional* — marriage converts eros into contract. The disruptive force becomes the founding ritual of property and inheritance. Freud's whole edifice rests here: civilization runs on redirected libido.
2. *Therapeutic* — eros reframed as "intimacy," "healthy sexuality," something to optimize with a couples counselor. Desire becomes a wellness metric. What was daemonic becomes a skill.
3. *Market* — the sharpest modern version. Byung-Chul Han (*The Agony of Eros*) argues eros requires an Other who resists you; consumer culture eliminates otherness entirely. Dating apps render desire as inventory. Porn delivers the erotic without the risk. Marcuse called this *repressive desublimation*: the system doesn't suppress sex, it floods you with a defanged version — permitted pleasure as a control mechanism.

**The paradox at the core.** Eros arguably *needs* obstacles — distance, prohibition, the unattainable — to exist at all (courtly love was an engine built on impossibility). So domestication doesn't merely constrain eros; it may starve it. Total permissiveness and total repression converge on the same dead point.

**A live case:** gay liberation. What began as a genuinely feral eros — cruising, transgression, existence outside the sanctioned family — was, within two generations, absorbed into marriage, monogamy, and a wedding registry. Whether that's victory or the most complete domestication on record is the whole debate between assimilationists and queer theorists (Bersani, Warner).

The open question your note points at: is domestication a betrayal of eros, or its only survivable form? Untamed eros destroys — Anna Karenina under the train. The tension may not be resolvable, which is why it keeps generating literature.
