# The Mechanisms

One table, as specified. Each entry is a single mechanism — one weighting, one process — followed by the environment it was tuned in and the conditions that flip its output. No flaw columns, no strength columns. The valence is always in the environment.

Grouped by function. Roughly forty entries. The grouping is for navigation; the mechanisms don't respect it — most fire together.

---

## I. Attention and Perception

**1. Novelty weighting.**
Attention allocates by change, not by importance. The mechanism assumes that what's new is what matters, because in a slow-changing environment that was almost always true — the new thing was the predator, the stranger, the fire. The tuning fails in any environment engineered to produce novelty on demand. A feed is a machine for generating false positives against this exact detector, and it wins every round because the detector can't be turned off, only exhausted. The same weighting that made humans the species that investigates is the one that makes them the species that scrolls.

**2. Threat priority.**
Negative signals get processing precedence — faster detection, deeper encoding, longer retention. The asymmetry is correct where the cost of a missed threat is death and the cost of a false alarm is wasted cortisol. It inverts wherever threats are informational rather than physical: news, criticism, ambiguous messages. A person can now consume more threat signals in a morning than an ancestor met in a year, none of them actionable. The mechanism responds to volume, not actionability, so it produces sustained arousal with no motor output — which is roughly the definition of anxiety.

**3. Goal-directed filtering.**
Attention suppresses everything not relevant to the current task. This is what makes sustained work possible at all — without it, cognition is a room with every radio on. The flip is that the filter has no channel for "important but off-topic." It will screen out the gorilla, the symptom, the relationship quietly failing in the background, with exactly the same efficiency it screens out noise. The people most capable of deep focus are structurally the most capable of missing the thing next to the thing.

**4. Pattern completion.**
Given partial data, the system fills in the rest from prior structure. This is not an occasional feature; it's how perception works at every level — you never see a whole face, you see fragments and complete them. The mechanism is agnostic about whether the pattern is real. Where the world is regular, completion is insight and expertise: the chess master seeing the position, the doctor seeing the diagnosis. Where the data is noise, the same operation runs anyway and returns conspiracies, gambler's streaks, and faces on Mars. There is no internal signal distinguishing the two cases. The check has to come from outside.

**5. Habituation.**
Repeated signals decay toward invisibility. Correct wherever a constant signal carries no news — the waterfall, the smell of your own house. It flips wherever a constant signal is the most important one: chronic risk, slow decline, ongoing injustice, the good thing you have. Habituation is why safety procedures erode, why abuse can normalize, and why gratitude requires deliberate effort — the mechanism is actively deleting the signal that gratitude needs.

**6. Agency detection.**
Ambiguous events get attributed to actors before accidents. The tuning is asymmetric on purpose: mistaking wind for a stalker costs a flinch; the reverse mistake costs everything. So the detector is set hot, and it stays hot in environments where most events genuinely are impersonal — markets, weather, bureaucracies, bad luck. The output is the persistent human sense that someone did this: gods behind storms, cabals behind prices, malice behind incompetence. The same detector, pointed at actual agents, is social intelligence. Pointed at systems, it's paranoia with a narrative.

## II. Memory

**7. Reconstruction.**
Memory is not playback; it's regeneration from compressed traces, filled in with current knowledge at retrieval time. The design is efficient — storing gist and rebuilding detail costs a fraction of storing everything — and it keeps memory usefully updated: what you know now improves the reconstruction of what happened then. The flip is that the seams don't show. A reconstructed memory feels identical to a recorded one, so confidence and accuracy decouple completely. Every retrieval is also a re-write, meaning the most-remembered events are the most-edited ones. The memories a person is surest of are the ones they've handled most.

**8. Availability weighting.**
Probability estimates run on retrieval ease: what comes to mind easily is judged common. In a world where you only heard about events near you, retrieval ease genuinely tracked local frequency — it was a good statistic. Mass media breaks the input assumption entirely. Now retrieval ease tracks what's *reported*, and reporting selects for the rare and vivid. The result is a systematic inversion: the more newsworthy an event, the rarer it is, the more probable it feels. People fear planes and drive cars. The mechanism is running correctly on corrupted data.

**9. Adaptive forgetting.**
Unretrieved information decays. This is maintenance, not failure — a memory that kept everything would be unsearchable, and forgetting is what keeps retrieval fast and relevance high. The flip is at the boundaries: forgetting is calibrated to usage frequency, not future importance. The rarely-needed critical fact — the emergency procedure, the anniversary, the lesson from the mistake you only made once — decays on the same schedule as trivia. Everything you've built to compensate, from calendars to checklists, is a prosthesis for this one tuning.

**10. Gist extraction.**
Memory keeps the meaning and discards the source and the wording. Efficient and usually right — the content matters more than the packaging. The flip is source amnesia: the claim survives, the credibility of where it came from doesn't. A debunked rumor, heard once, becomes a free-floating "I read somewhere that…" six months later, with the debunking gone and the claim intact. Repetition then does the rest — familiar claims feel true because familiarity is the gist system's only receipt. This single mechanism is most of why misinformation works and corrections don't.

**11. Peak-end compression.**
Experiences are stored as two samples — the most intense moment and the final moment — with duration nearly discarded. As compression, it's defensible: peaks tell you the range of what a situation can do, endings tell you how it resolves, and those are the decision-relevant facts. The flip is everywhere duration actually matters. The mechanism will rate a longer pain with a gentle ending as better than a shorter pain, will remember a good vacation with one bad final day as a bad vacation, and will let one strong ending redeem or ruin years. People then optimize their lives for remembered experience rather than lived experience, and the two are different quantities.

## III. Learning and Prediction

**12. Fast aversive learning.**
One bad experience can install a permanent avoidance. Correct asymmetry: the food that made you sick once should be avoided forever, because the sample size that matters is one. The flip is that the mechanism can't distinguish signal from coincidence — it tags whatever was present. One bad public speaking experience, one betrayal, one failure, and the tag is installed with the same permanence as the poison tag. Extinction is slow by design; the system was built assuming false negatives kill and false positives merely inconvenience. In a modern life, the false positives compound into a shrinking map of where you're willing to go.

**13. Prediction-error learning.**
The system learns from surprise: expected outcomes teach nothing, unexpected ones drive updates. This is the correct algorithm — it's the same one machine learning converged on — and it's why expertise is efficient: the expert only processes deviations. The flip has two faces. **13. Prediction-error learning.** *(continued)*
The flip has two faces. First, no error, no learning — a person whose environment confirms their model stops updating entirely, and confirmation can be manufactured by simply not going where the surprises are. Comfort is a learning-rate of zero. Second, the mechanism updates on surprise regardless of whether the surprise generalizes. One vivid exception can overwrite a correct base rate, because the exception generated a large error signal and the base rate, being expected, generated none. The system is tuned to a world where surprises were representative. Ours manufactures unrepresentative surprises industrially.

**14. Imitation with prestige weighting.**
Copy the successful before understanding why they succeed. This is the core of cumulative culture, and its power is precisely that it doesn't require understanding — a child can inherit ten thousand years of tested technique without deriving any of it. The tuning assumes prestige correlates with competence in the copied domain. It flips wherever prestige is achievable without competence: celebrity, virality, inherited status, confidence itself. The mechanism then copies the signal instead of the substance — the diet of the actor, the opinion of the athlete, the swagger of the fraud. It also can't distinguish causal features from incidental ones, so it copies the whole package: the successful person's method and their superstitions arrive together.

**15. Overpractice automation.**
Repeated sequences compile into procedures that run without attention. This is the entire basis of skill — driving, typing, speech itself. Attention is freed for the novel part of the task because the routine part is handled below awareness. The flip is that compiled procedures are nearly write-protected. The error practiced into the routine is now automatic too, and correcting it costs more than the original learning did, because the automation has to be fought at every execution. Ten years of experience is sometimes one year of experience, compiled, and then run nine more times.

**16. Causal narrative construction.**
Events get bound into cause-effect stories automatically, before any evidence review. Stories are the native format of human prediction — compressed, memorable, transmissible — and a decent causal story is genuinely better than a list of facts, because it tells you what to expect next. The flip is that the mechanism produces a story from *any* input, including noise, and produces exactly one, and produces it with a feeling of understanding attached. The feeling arrives whether or not the story is true. Markets get narrated daily by this mechanism — every price movement receives a cause by evening — and the narration is indistinguishable, from inside, from actual comprehension.

## IV. Valuation and Decision

**17. Loss aversion.**
Losses weigh roughly twice as much as equivalent gains. Near subsistence this is correct arithmetic: a loss that drops you below the survival line can't be offset by a symmetric gain, because you're dead. The asymmetry encodes a floor. It flips wherever there's slack — savings, insurance, diversification, any modern buffer — because with a floor no longer nearby, the correct weighting is symmetric and the mechanism keeps refusing good bets. It also makes people fight harder to keep than to acquire, which stabilizes possessions and relationships and also keeps them in failing versions of both.

**18. Hyperbolic discounting.**
The near future is weighted enormously over the far future — not smoothly, but with a cliff right at "now." In a volatile environment this is rational: a promised future reward has to be discounted by the probability the promiser, or you, won't be there to complete the trade. The steep curve prices in a dangerous world. It flips in a stable one, where the far future reliably arrives and compounding rewards those who can wait. Every savings crisis, every deadline crunch, every "diet starts Monday" is this one curve, run in an environment where the volatility it was pricing no longer exists.

**19. Anchoring.**
Estimates start from whatever number is present and adjust from there — insufficiently. As a strategy it's sound: a nearby number is usually relevant information, and adjusting from a starting point is cheaper than estimating from nothing. The flip is that the mechanism doesn't check the anchor's relevance. An arbitrary number, a first offer, a sticker price — all get treated as information. Anyone who controls the first number in a negotiation controls the range of the negotiation, and the mechanism offers no internal warning that this is happening.

**20. Sunk cost weighting.**
Prior investment increases commitment. As a social mechanism, this is a feature, not a bug: creatures that abandon projects at every dip are exploitable and unreliable, and a reputation for follow-through has real value. Persistence past the rational quit-point is also what finishes hard things — most worthwhile projects have a valley where quitting looks correct. The flip is that the mechanism can't distinguish a valley from a hole. The same weighting that finishes dissertations continues wars. The information needed to tell them apart — is future return still positive? — is exactly the input the mechanism ignores in favor of past cost.

**21. Zero-risk preference.**
Eliminating a risk entirely feels categorically better than reducing a larger risk by a greater amount. The tuning makes sense for cognitive budgeting: a risk at zero can be deleted from monitoring, freeing attention permanently, while a reduced risk still costs vigilance. The flip is in any portfolio of risks, where the arithmetic says reduce the big one and the mechanism says purge the small one. Safety policy, medicine, and personal life all show the signature: enormous resources spent driving small risks to zero while large ones sit at "reduced-ish."

**22. Effort justification.**
Things cost more, therefore worth more — the valuation runs backward from the price paid. This stabilizes commitment: the group you suffered to join, the skill you bled for, the relationship you fought through, all get retroactive value that keeps you invested. Hazing works on this mechanism; so does every hard apprenticeship. The flip is that it valorizes cost itself, independent of return. The mechanism will defend the terrible decision *because* it was expensive, and will rate the identical wine higher at the higher price. It's the internal auditor who approves anything, as long as it was over budget.

## V. Social Cognition

**23. Reciprocity tracking.**
A running ledger of favors given and received, maintained automatically for every relationship. This is the infrastructure of cooperation among non-kin — the thing that lets strangers trade, neighbors share, societies exist at all. The flip is that the ledger can't be turned off in relationships where it doesn't belong. Applied to intimacy, it converts gifts into debts and generosity into positioning. Applied by the other party — the free gift before the ask, the unsolicited favor — it's the most exploited mechanism in commerce. And it's asymmetric in bookkeeping: your own contributions are recorded at full price, others' at a discount, so every shared household contains two ledgers that don't reconcile.

**24. Ingroup preference.**
Trust, resources, and benefit-of-the-doubt flow preferentially to the marked group. The mechanism is the foundation of every functioning team, family, and community — cooperation requires bounded trust, and the boundary is the mechanism. The flip is that the marker is arbitrary and the mechanism doesn't know it. It was tuned for groups of people you actually knew, where group membership carried real information about reciprocity. It fires identically for jersey colors, accents, and assigned teams in a lab. The dose makes the poison: at low intensity it's community, at high intensity it's the mechanism history books are mostly about.

**25. Reputation management.**
A continuous background process modeling how you appear to others and adjusting behavior toward the model. In a small group with permanent membership, this is simply the cost of doing business — reputation was the only currency that mattered, and the process earned its overhead. The flip is twofold. First, the audience model doesn't scale down: it runs at full intensity for strangers you'll never see again, generating embarrassment in front of people whose opinion carries zero consequence. Second, it doesn't scale up: performing for an audience of millions, the mechanism treats every viewer as a village member whose judgment matters, which is why public criticism online produces distress calibrated for exile.

**26. Cheater detection.**
Reasoning about social rules runs on dedicated, high-performance machinery — people who fail abstract logic problems solve the identical problem instantly when it's framed as catching a rule-breaker. The specialization is honest about what logic was for: policing cooperation. The flip is a hair trigger with a confirmation habit. The mechanism generates suspects readily, and once a suspect exists, the general-purpose confirmation machinery (entry 31) takes over the case. It also fires on rule-breaking as such, independent of harm — the visceral response to a victimless violation is this mechanism insisting that the rule itself is the victim.

**27. Intentionality attribution.**
Others' behavior is explained by their character; your own by your circumstances. The asymmetry falls out of the data each side has: you observe your situation from inside and others' behavior from outside, so the explanation follows the visible evidence. As a fast model of other agents, "behavior reveals disposition" is often accurate enough. The flip is systematic and unforgiving: it converts others' bad days into character verdicts while dissolving your own identical behavior into context. Every feud is two parties running this mechanism at each other, each holding a dossier of the other's dispositions and a diary of their own extenuating circumstances.

**28. Conformity to consensus.**
When the group's judgment conflicts with your perception, the group gets weight — often enough weight to override the perception. This is usually correct: many eyes beat two, and most of what any person knows arrived through this channel, not through direct verification. Deferring to consensus is how finite creatures get access to more evidence than they can gather. The flip is that the mechanism counts *expressed* opinions, not independent ones. A cascade — where each person defers to the people before them — presents as consensus while containing almost no information. The mechanism can't audit its inputs, so manufactured agreement, loud minorities, and bot swarms all get counted as many eyes.

**29. Status tracking.**
A dedicated system continuously computes relative position — who defers to whom, who's rising, where you stand. In a primate hierarchy, this was survival-critical information updated in real time, and the system is correspondingly sensitive and always on. The flip is that it defines its own comparison set by proximity and visibility, not relevance. It was tuned for a village where the comparison set was a hundred people and standing near the top was achievable. Pointed at a feed, it selects the global maximum of every dimension as the reference point, and returns "low status" for essentially everyone, forever. The system is functioning perfectly. The input is insane.

**30. Empathic simulation.**
Others' states are understood by partially running them — their pain on your pain machinery, their joy on yours. This is the mechanism that makes others' experiences *matter* rather than merely being known; it's the difference between reading a casualty figure and flinching. The flip is in its range and its aperture. Range: it runs on vividness, not magnitude — one visible child outweighs a statistical million, so it allocates moral attention by narrative quality. Aperture: it simulates most easily what's most similar to you, so it quietly reproduces the ingroup boundary (entry 24) and calls the result compassion. And run continuously against unfixable suffering, it damages the operator — burnout is this mechanism with no motor output, same structure as entry 2.

## VI. Self-Model and Belief

**31. Confirmation-weighted search.**
Evidence gathering is directed by the current hypothesis: the search looks where confirmation would be. As a search strategy this is defensible — testing a hypothesis by checking its predictions is how you use a hypothesis at all, and random search is expensive. The flip is that the mechanism never spontaneously runs the disconfirming search. It will check whether the theory's predictions occur; it will not check whether they'd occur anyway. Left alone, every belief accumulates support, because the belief is directing the accumulation. Science is, structurally, just this mechanism with a mandatory adversarial step bolted on — the bolt-on being necessary is the whole finding.

**32. Cognitive dissonance resolution.**
Conflicts between beliefs, or between belief and action, generate discomfort that demands resolution. The mechanism enforces internal consistency, which is genuinely valuable — an agent whose beliefs contradict each other can't plan, and the discomfort is the error signal that forces the merge. The flip is in which element yields. Actions are done and public; beliefs are private and editable. So the resolution almost always edits the belief to fit the act, not the reverse. The person who behaved badly doesn't conclude they behaved badly; they conclude the victim deserved it, and the conclusion arrives feeling like discovery. The consistency enforcer works — it just enforces consistency with the least revisable element, which is the past.

**33. Positive self-illusion.**
The self-model runs a few degrees above accurate — abilities, prospects, control, all modestly inflated. The calibration is functional, not broken: agents with accurate self-models under-attempt, and the expected value of attempting hard things is positive even at low individual success rates, *if* someone attempts them. The inflation is the attempt-generator. Depression's "realism" is partly this mechanism offline — accurate and immobilized. The flip is dose-dependent and domain-dependent: mild inflation drives startups and asks-for-dates; the same mechanism at higher gain drives overleveraged bets, unprepared summit attempts, and the confidence of the incompetent — who lack not only skill but the skill to detect its absence, so the mechanism's output goes unaudited precisely where it's most wrong.

**34. Identity-belief fusion.**
Beliefs held long enough, or acquired socially enough, get absorbed into the self-model — challenges to the belief then process as attacks on the person. This makes cultures possible: values that can be argued away in an afternoon can't anchor a community, and fusion is what gives commitments their durability. Martyrdom, loyalty, and integrity all run on beliefs that have become load-bearing. The flip is that the fusion is content-blind. It protects a person's deepest values and their team's quarterback opinions with the same machinery, and once fused, evidence against the belief is processed by threat systems, not truth systems. The belief has stopped being a map and started being a wall.

**35. Confabulated agency.**
The self-model explains its own behavior by generating reasons after the fact — introspection reports a plausible story, not the actual mechanism. This is the press office, not the government. The design is efficient: real causes of behavior are distributed, numerous, and mostly inaccessible, and a coherent narrative self is more useful for planning and social explanation than an accurate mess would be. The flip is that the press office believes its own releases. The reasons feel like causes, so people defend the confabulation as if it were the mechanism, and self-knowledge becomes the practiced production of plausible stories about a stranger. Every mechanism in this table operates behind this one, which is why none of them feel like they apply to you.

**36. Moral self-licensing.**
Good acts accumulate in an internal moral account, and a positive balance permits withdrawals. As bookkeeping it's honest — a moral self-image tolerating zero deviation would be paralyzed, and the account smooths behavior over time rather than demanding perfection per act. The flip is that the account pays interest on *symbols*. Cheap moral acts — the statement, the donation button, the visible gesture — credit the account at full value, and the license then funds real harms. The mechanism explains the specific texture of hypocrisy: it's worst not in the amoral, who keep no account, but in the actively virtuous, whose account is always in surplus.

## VII. Emotion as Computation

**37. Anxiety as pre-computation.**
Worry is the simulation of bad futures in advance — a rehearsal system, running failure modes before they're live. Functioning correctly, it's preparation: the worried presenter checks the projector. The flip is when simulation decouples from action. The mechanism has no completion signal other than "problem addressed," so unaddressable problems — distant, hypothetical, other people's — put it in an infinite loop, rehearsing without ever discharging into preparation. Modern information supply provides unlimited unaddressable problems. The mechanism treats each one as a rehearsal request.

**38. Anger as leverage enforcement.**
Anger is a bargaining state: it recalibrates others' treatment of you by credibly signaling that mistreatment will now be expensive. The physiology is the credibility — visible arousal proves the threat isn't bluff. Within relationships that continue, it works; treatment genuinely improves after calibrated anger. The flip is that the mechanism assumes the target can hear the signal and adjust. Aimed at institutions, traffic, software, or the past, the display cost is paid and no recalibration occurs — the leverage mechanism fires into targets that hold no negotiable position. And the credibility feature becomes the liability: the arousal that proves you're serious is the same arousal that outlives the incident.

**39. Sadness as disengagement.**
Sadness withdraws energy from a lost objective — it's the demotivation that stops you from pursuing what's gone. The function is reallocation: attachment to the unattainable is expensive, and the low-energy state forces model revision before new investment. Grief is the system rewriting a world-model that had another person load-bearing in it, and the slowness is proportional to the rewrite. The flip is when disengagement generalizes — designed to release one objective, the state can spread to all objectives, and the reallocation pause becomes the destination. Depression is, in part, this mechanism unable to find the boundary of the loss.

**40. Disgust as contamination logic.**
A rejection system with distinctive rules: contact contaminates, contamination is permanent, dose is irrelevant. For pathogens, these rules are correct — one touch of the carcass genuinely is enough, and washing genuinely doesn't restore trust. The flip is that the mechanism was recruited for social and moral judgment with its rules intact. Applied to people, it produces the specific signature of dehumanization: the outgroup as vermin, the sinner as unclean, the trace as total — one drop, one ancestor, one act, and the person is categorically contaminated. Moral disgust feels like moral perception, but it's running quarantine logic, and quarantine logic has no concept of redemption.

## The Pattern Under the Table

Read down the environment column and one thing repeats. Almost every flip has the same shape: **the mechanism is fine; the input distribution changed.** Availability weighting was correct until news. Status tracking was correct until feeds. Threat priority was correct until the threats became unactionable. The discounting curve was correct until the future became reliable.

The mechanisms were tuned against a world with specific statistics — small groups, local information, real scarcity, representative surprises — and the tuning is mostly excellent *for that world*. What's changed isn't the machinery. It's that the modern environment is increasingly built by people who have read this table, pointed at people who haven't. Every feed, every price tag, every free sample, every outrage cycle is one of these entries, located and monetized.

Which gives the table its actual use. Not self-knowledge in the introspective sense — entry 35 explains why that mostly doesn't work; you can't feel these firing, and the reasons you generate will be confabulated anyway. The use is environmental: you can't retune the mechanisms, but you can choose their inputs. Every entry above is exploitable through its inputs, and defensible the same way. That's the single actionable finding, and it's the same one at every row.
