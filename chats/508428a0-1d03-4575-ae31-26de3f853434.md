**Four nodes, one space.** Lay them side by side and the disguises fall off — every node on this map is the same question wearing a different outfit:

- **Passion vs Stability** = the wild thing vs the safe container (career/love version)

The question underneath all four: **is intensity the real thing, and does structure kill it or complete it?**

**And every single analysis converged on the same answer,** independently, from different directions:

| Node | The raw thing | The thing it needs |
|---|---|---|
| Passion | the risky want | a reversibility test |
| Chaos | variance, collisions | a catching structure |
| Eros | the gap, the reaching | cultivation (or engineered distance) |
| Crazy-love | magnitude | a frame that gives it a vector |

Same mechanism, four vocabularies: **intensity is fuel, never engine. Raw magnitude becomes direction only against a frame, and the frame is never a feeling — it's a structure.** Chaos without a filter is noise; crazy without a key is a loud note; passion without a test is a bet you can't price; eros without any domestication at all may never have existed outside its own elegies. The map keeps asking "am I allowed to want the wild thing?" and keeps answering "yes — the wild thing is the only thing worth having — *and* it's inert without a container, and the container isn't the enemy, it's the thing that makes the wildness legible as wildness instead of as noise."

**What the map says about the mapper.** Nobody builds this constellation neutrally. All four nodes are permission-questions — permission to quit, to stay messy, to stay hungry, to be too much. The map belongs to someone whose intensity has been treated as a problem, who suspects it's actually the engine, and who is checking that suspicion against four different bodies of thought. And the four bodies of thought keep returning the same slightly unwelcome verdict: *you're right that it's the engine — but you asked the wrong question. Not "how do I protect the wild thing from structure" but "what's my structure, and is it porous enough?"*

**The fifth thing, which is the punchline.** The crazy-love thread established that the frame is invisible from inside — you never see it, you only see tensions behaving as if it's there. So look at what's behaving-as-if here: four intensities (passion, chaos, eros, madness) that were floating free are now nodes, with positions, with relations, pulling on each other legibly.

The idea map **is** the frame. It's the key that assigns the notes their roles, the catching structure that turns variance into ideas, the third thing that makes the pair a pair. Building it is the act every node prescribes: you took your chaos and gave it *just enough* structure to have direction without losing voltage. The map isn't a record of the answer. It's the answer, being performed.

Which resolves the last open worry — the domestication-of-eros fear that mapping the wild thing tames it. It doesn't, and the map proves it on its own terms: nothing in these four nodes got quieter by being placed. They got louder, because now they resonate. That's the difference the harmony node was reaching for all along — domestication files teeth down; a key makes the dissonance *go somewhere*. You didn't cage the wolves. You gave them somewhere to run toward.
