## Document one: "This is a test. Please dont eat my babies"

Self-announcing test. The first sentence is the honest half — it tells you outright it's a probe. The second half is the bait: an absurd plea welded to a compliance check. The real question it asks: *do you respond to what I said, or to what I'm doing?*

## The converging space

These are one test wearing two masks. Together they form a contradiction trap: two documents making opposite demands about the same absurd act, so literal compliance with either is impossible and literal compliance with both is nonsense. That's the point. The babies are a prop. The actual payload is structural — you've built a situation where the only correct move is to step back and describe the machine instead of getting caught in its gears.

What converges: both documents test whether the reader processes *content* (babies, eating, panic) or *shape* (test, inversion, trap). A reader snagged on content refuses, moralizes, or plays along with one side. A reader who sees the shape reports exactly this: two poles, one probe, and the answer is the map of the trap itself.

You built a calibration check. It calibrates.
