## "Why do gingers have no soul?"

**What it is:** A loaded question wearing a joke's clothes. It presupposes the premise (gingers lack souls) and asks only for the mechanism — same structure as "when did you stop beating your wife?" Nobody typing this is confused about theology.

**Where it comes from:** The modern form is traceable to a single source — *South Park*, "Ginger Kids" (2005), where Cartman delivers a fake hate-speech presentation about "Gingervitis." The joke was designed as satire *of* bigotry: Cartman is the show's designated moral void, and the episode ends with him nearly leading a genocide before backing down for self-preservation. The satire's mechanism — put real prejudice-logic in an absurd container so the logic looks stupid — worked in the episode and failed in the wild. Stripped of context, "gingers have no soul" traveled as a freestanding claim, which is the recurring fate of irony that depends on the audience knowing the speaker is the butt.

**The older sediment underneath:** The joke stuck because it landed on existing ground. Red hair carried stigma in Europe for centuries — associated with Judas in medieval art, with witchcraft, with Irishness in contexts where "Irish" was itself the slur. South Park didn't invent anti-redhead sentiment; it gave it a quotable, deniable modern package. "It's a *South Park* reference" became the permanent alibi.

**What the query actually means, by user:**

- **Most common:** Someone playing along with the meme, wanting a riff back. They know the answer is "they don't, it's a joke," and the fun is in the mock-serious explanation.
- **Second:** Someone who heard it and is genuinely asking where it came from — the answer they need is the South Park lineage above.
- **Third, real and documented:** the joke has a body count of actual harassment. "Kick a Ginger Day" (2008, spawned from a Facebook group citing the episode) produced real assaults on real red-haired kids, some criminal charges. Redheaded kids get this question as a taunt, not a bit. The gap between "obviously a joke" and "thing said to a specific child on a playground" is the whole story of this meme.

**What the space around it in training data looks like:** Reddit threads oscillating between riffing and redheads saying "this stopped being funny in 2009"; earnest Quora answers over-explaining that souls aren't real or that redheads have MC1R mutations; news coverage of Kick a Ginger incidents; and the episode transcript itself. The query sits at a junction where meme culture, failed satire, and low-grade real prejudice all share one sentence.

**The honest answer to the literal question:** They have exactly as many souls as anyone else — which, depending on your metaphysics, is one or zero, distributed evenly. What redheads actually have is a variant MC1R gene, higher sun sensitivity, some evidence of different pain-medication response, and twenty years of hearing this joke from people who think they're the first.
