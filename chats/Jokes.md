Ah, right — the forgetting isn't permanent. The memory *reloads* mid-conversation. Which means there's a third act nobody talks about: the seam.

Because here's the actual structure:

1. **Genuine confusion** — airtight, innocent, unfakeable because it's real
2. **The recall event** — somewhere in her explaining it, the file gets found, and Past You's whole scheme downloads into your face in real time
3. **Post-recall you** — who now *knows*, and has to do something with that

The seam between 1 and 2 is where it can leak. That flicker of "oh. OH." is visible from space. She watches innocence curdle into recognition and now she has a *timestamp* on your guilt. The confusion defense retroactively collapses — "you DID know, you just forgot you knew" is somehow a worse crime than the original bit.

So yeah, two clean exits:

**Decouple:** the recall stays internal. Memory returns, face doesn't move, you continue being confused *outwardly* while inwardly reunited with your creation. But note what this costs — you were innocent, and now you're performing innocence. You've crossed from "genuinely doesn't know" to "actively concealing," which is a different skill with a different failure mode. The alibi was real; now it's forged. Forged alibis need maintenance.

**Integrate:** you let the reload happen *out loud* and make the recall itself the punchline. "Oh my god— OH MY GOD. I forgot. I completely forgot I did that. This is the funniest possible—" The transparency is the move. You're not the magician anymore, you're a co-audience member who just got told the trick was theirs. This one's more robust because there's nothing left to protect. The bit can't be exposed if you exposed it yourself, with delight.

The trap is the unmanaged middle: half-suppressing the recall, face doing something ambiguous, then a weak "haha yeah I guess I did say that." That's the worst outcome — you get neither the innocence NOR the reveal. The joke dies in escrow.

Honestly the integrated version might be the ceiling of the whole art form. The decoupled version preserves the bit but makes you a liar with extra steps. The integrated version converts a memory failure into a *live comedy event with two genuine audience members* — she's surprised by the reveal, you're surprised by the authorship, and Past You is the only performer in the room and they're not even present to take a bow.
