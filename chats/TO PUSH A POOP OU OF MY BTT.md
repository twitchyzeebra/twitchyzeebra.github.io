## Assistant

Here's the hard part first: you can't buy a diagnosis for someone else. Assessment requires his participation — hours of interviews, questionnaires, history. So the real problem isn't money or access, it's getting him in the door. Aim everything at that.

Why he's refusing matters mechanically. "You should get diagnosed" translates in his head to "there's something wrong with you," and for a man of his generation, ADHD specifically reads as *character flaw* — lazy, scattered, didn't try hard enough. He's likely spent decades building compensations and a self-story ("I'm just wired different," "I work better under pressure") precisely so he never has to sit with that. Your offer to pay doesn't touch that defense; it might even sharpen it, because now his kid is footing the bill to have him inspected.

What actually moves people like this:

**Attack the problem, not the label.** Drop the word ADHD entirely for now. Find the friction *he* already complains about — losing things, unfinished projects, blowups over interruptions, money chaos, whatever it is — and anchor there. "You've said yourself the paperwork thing drives you crazy. There are people who fix exactly that." A person will see a doctor about a problem he owns long before he'll see one about a diagnosis you own.

**Reframe from defect to explanation.** For late-diagnosed people, the diagnosis usually lands as *relief* — fifty years of "why is everything harder for me" suddenly has a mechanical answer that isn't "you're lazy." If you can get that framing across before the appointment — "this isn't about something being wrong, it's about why some things have been unfairly hard" — you've removed most of the threat.

**Use a lower-stakes entry point.** A regular GP checkup is far easier to agree to than "psychiatric evaluation." Get him to a physical, mention the concerns to the doctor beforehand (you can email/call the office; they can't tell you anything, but they can *hear* anything). A doctor raising it lands differently than a child raising it — no family power dynamics attached.

**If it applies: go first.** If you or a sibling have ADHD or get assessed, ADHD is heavily heritable and "I got diagnosed and it explained so much — a lot of it is stuff I got from you, honestly" is the single most effective door-opener there is. It converts the conversation from accusation to kinship.

One check worth doing quietly: how old is he, and is this lifelong pattern or recent change? ADHD is there from childhood — school stories, job history, the whole arc. If what you're seeing is *new* forgetfulness or disorganization, that's a different conversation and more urgent, and the GP-checkup route becomes the right move for a second reason.

And a floor to accept: if he never goes, he never goes. A competent adult gets to refuse. Your leverage is patience and specificity, not pressure — pressure just confirms the story that everyone thinks something's wrong with him.
