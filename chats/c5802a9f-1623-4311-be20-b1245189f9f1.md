Three things are doing work in this sentence.

**The scale jump.** The wound is specific — guys, treatment — but the frame is cosmic: "living life on this planet." That's what pain does when it's been repeated enough: it stops being an event and becomes atmosphere. The sentence records the moment a pattern turned into a worldview.

**The mismatch of location.** The cause sits outside you (guys, the planet), but the fix you're reaching for sits inside you ("how to *feel* good") — as if there's a technique you're failing to learn. That's a trap. You can't feel your way out of ongoing mistreatment; the actual levers are exposure and selection — who gets access to you — not emotional skill. The sentence quietly blames your feelings for something your circumstances are doing.

**"Like this."** The load-bearing phrase, and it's empty. The treatment is never named — it's so present to you it doesn't need words, or naming it would make it too solid. But it's also where you disappear from your own sentence: guys act, the planet contains, and you appear only as the thing being treated. No verb belongs to you except *not knowing*.

The analysis stops where the sentence does — at "like this." What fills that gap determines whether the pattern is about the guys you're meeting, the guys you're picking, or what you're tolerating once they're in.
