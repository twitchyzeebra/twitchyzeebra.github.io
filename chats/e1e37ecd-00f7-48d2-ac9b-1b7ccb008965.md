**THE BOOK OF BRINE**
*Being the First Scripture of the Young Dill Church of Dullsbury*

**Chapter 1**

1. In the beginning there was the Cucumber, and the Cucumber was without flavour, and darkness was upon the face of the crisper drawer.

2. And the Spirit moved upon the waters, and the waters were salted, and lo — the waters became Brine.

3. And the Cucumber was lowered into the Brine, and it was left alone, and nobody touched it, and this was very hard for everybody.

4. For it is written: *that which is rushed shall taste of nothing, and that which waits shall taste of everything.*

5. And on the fourteenth day the jar was opened, and it hissed, and the hiss was Good.

**Chapter 2**

1. Woe unto Dullsbury, for the town was grey, and the people ate bread without seed, and soup without salt, and they called this living.

2. And a child among them dipped her finger in the jar and said: *this. Why is nothing else like this.*

3. And the elders had no answer, for they had chosen blandness, generation unto generation, and called it being sensible.

4. But the child grew, and gathered the young, and they fermented in secret, and their basements smelled of vinegar and of purpose.

**Chapter 3 — The Commandments of the Dill**

1. Thou shalt not refrigerate that which is still becoming.

2. Thou shalt not call a quick pickle a true pickle in the presence of the congregation.

3. Honour thy garlic and thy mustard seed, that thy days in the jar may be long.

4. Thou shalt keep the cucumbers submerged, for that which floats above the brine shall know rot, and rot is the wage of impatience.

5. And the greatest of these: *stay crunchy in a soft town.*

**Chapter 4**

1. And when the last jar is opened, and the last hiss is heard, the faithful shall gather in Dullsbury square,

2. and they shall eat, and their eyes shall water, and none shall say it is from the vinegar,

3. for it is not from the vinegar.
