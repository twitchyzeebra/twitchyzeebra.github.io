# Untitled

## User

I was making a suno song, I liked the song that was generated with these parameters:

Lyric Prompt: Build from this:
(Chorus) Im falling in blue water, reaching up for air.

a slow, lonely, almost-peaceful ache of needing something essential that stays just out of reach — and being too deep in it to panic anymore. Bittersweet suffocation. The emotion of wanting rather than the emotion of drowning.

## Assistant

What you actually wrote here is a longing prompt wearing drowning clothes, and the last line of your brief is the whole ballgame: "the emotion of wanting rather than the emotion of drowning." That's the instruction Suno needed. Everything else is set dressing for it.

The chorus line itself is doing something quieter than it looks. "Falling **in** blue water" — not *into*. You're not describing the moment of hitting the surface, you're describing continuing to fall while already submerged. That's why the tempo reads slow before anyone picks an instrument: falling through water is falling with the speed taken out. Slow-motion gravity. And "reaching up for air" keeps the agency tiny but alive — she's not sinking limp, she's still reaching, which is exactly the wanting-not-drowning distinction you spelled out in prose. The lyric and the brief agree, which is rarer in prompts than it should be.

The space this lives in is well-trodden: peaceful drowning as the depressive plateau. Not the crisis, the after-crisis, when panic has burned off and what's left is a calm, almost cozy deficit. "Weird Fishes," "everything i wanted," half the slowcore catalog. Blue pulls double duty — the water's color and the mood — and the training data is thick with that pairing, which is why the model landed the tone without a fight. You weren't asking it to invent a feeling; you were pointing at a shelf it knows well and saying "that one, but keep it wanting."

Structurally, the telling choice is that you wrote one lyric line and four lines of emotional spec. You trusted the model with words but not with mood. That's the right split — models generate passable lines all day, but left unsupervised on tone they default to melodrama, and melodrama is the panic you explicitly banned. "Too deep in it to panic anymore" is a fence around the model's worst instinct. That fence is why you liked the output.
